# Midnights Manner — Peoples of the Long Dusk

Nobody here is evil. Everybody is hungry, tired, or owed. The valley runs on grudges and favors in about equal measure.

## The Mannerfolk (us)

Settlers, runaways, second sons, tired soldiers. Twelve trades plus the first five hands. They came because there were beds and bread, and they stay because the bell rings for them by name.

- **Want:** a full granary, a mended wall, one quiet night in three.
- **Hold:** the bowl valley, the stream, the Manor bell.
- **Grudge:** against toll-gangs who taxed them on the way in — most Mannerfolk paid Joren's chain once and never forgot it.
- **Soft spot:** any failed-manner refugee with tools in hand. Beds before bodies is law here.

## The Westroad Toll-Gangs (hold the western road)

Two crews share the Timber Line road by an uneasy rota: **Kess Longaxe's** polemen and **Dren Salt's** carters-turned-tollmen. They mend bridges, fell barricades, and charge for passage — in moonstone, flour, or labor.

- **Want:** steady tribute without a fight. A fight costs men they cannot replace.
- **Hold:** the western road itself — the only wheeled way in. That is why every raid comes from the west.
- **Why they raid:** when tribute dries up, young members freelance as night-probers. The gangs deny it and take a cut all the same.
- **Alliance:** they will sell the Manner road-news and spare nails at a markup, and they hate poachers who skip the toll.

## The Ashwood Poachers (raid because the woods quit feeding them)

Timber crews from two failed clearings up the treeline. Their hearths went cold in Dusk Year 39 — dry wells, pale soil, a bell that cracked. Now they cut our timber green, lift sheep, and pry moonstone from shallow diggings.

- **Want:** a hearth of their own again, or at least a winter's stores.
- **Why they raid:** not hate — arithmetic. A night's lifting feeds a family a month. They break fast and run early because they have children waiting in cold camps.
- **Grudge:** against the Manner for turning away their kin in Dusk Year 40, when there were no beds. True enough. The hall book records it.
- **Opening:** the mooncleric has twice ransomed captives with bread instead of chain. Some poachers now tap the Signal Watchfire twice before a raid — a warning, of sorts.

## The Grey Market Caravans (trade, never fight)

Traveling traders out of the Lantern Towns down the road. Six wagons, mule-drawn, lamp-hung. Never seen on-map — they exist as trading-post offers and notice-board rumors.

- **Want:** moonstone small and clean, timber hard and blue-veined, stories they can sell two valleys over.
- **Bring:** salt, nails, seed barley, news, lamp oil, and once a year a new bell-clapper.
- **Rule:** fair but sharp. They will not carry weapons for you, will not starve a village for leverage, and will absolutely charge you for the rope they throw you.
- **Alliance:** they favor the Manner because we pay on time. They fear the toll-gangs because the gangs take the same cut every trip.

## The Lantern Towns (far, fat, nervous)

Three walled market towns two days down the road, past the Sunken Steeple. Lit whole streets. They buy everything and promise nothing.

- **Want:** moonstone for lamps, ward-bells for walls, no refugees.
- **Tension:** they turned away Mannerfolk grandparents in the First Nights, and now pay premium for Manner timber. Nobody forgot. Everybody still sells.

## The Moonwardens (keep charts, give work, own nothing)

Scattered wayfinders, scholars, and moonclerics with old survey-charts and bell-codes. They gave the Manner its founding chart and its bell-tune. They give quests, never armies.

- **Want:** every lit window kept lit. One more hearth on the chart.
- **Rule:** they will teach the standing-wall drill, bless a wall, copy a chart — they will not hold a spear on it.
- **Grudge:** none, which makes everyone slightly suspicious of them.

## The shape of it

The toll-gangs tax the Markets. The Markets pay the Manner. The Manner feeds poacher captives. The poachers warn the watchfire. The Wardens write it all down and call it peace. It holds until somebody goes hungry — then it gets renegotiated at night, along the Timber Line, with poles and traps.
