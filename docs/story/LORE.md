# Midnights Manner — Lore Bible (Expanded)

All original writing for this game. No copied settings, characters, or place names from other works. If a name sounds familiar, it is coincidence — rename it.

This document is the living canon. Keep it consistent with every system, quest, mission, and sprite. Future content must respect the hard rules and open questions below.

---

## 1. Core Premise

The sun went thin a generation ago and never came fully back. In the **Long Dusk**, the frontier valleys sit under a permanent moonlit night: crops grow pale and sweet, timber grows hard and blue-veined, and the old roads belong to whoever holds a lit window. Settlers follow the rumor of **moonstone** — gold that holds moonlight — and build walled manners around a single great **Manor Hall**. Our village is one such manner: one lit window in a dark valley, and keeping it lit is the whole game.

**Tone pillars (non-negotiable):**
- Quiet frontier hope, not grim horror. Danger is real, but the fantasy is *keeping the lights on together*.
- Practical and tactile: bread, timber, nails, watch-rosters, mended walls. Magic is rare and small — a blessing on a wall, a charm on a lamp. Never a substitute for work.
- Humor is dry and neighborly. Raiders are desperate, not evil incarnate; villagers grumble, then show up.
- Nobody gives up. Every defeat ends with salvage and rebuilding, never annihilation.

**Hard rules (what can never happen):**
- The sun does not return. That ends the setting.
- No gods hand out victories. No resurrection of the dead — troops *revive* because they were dragged inside the walls, not raised.
- No empires, dark lords, or world-ending prophecies. All conflict is human-scale: hunger, tolls, failed manners, grudges.
- Magic never replaces work. Ward-light steadies; it does not smite.
- The home village is never permanently destroyed by an expedition. Rewards and scars come home only on explicit return.

**Known truth vs actual truth:**
- What the village believes: the Thinning was a slow weather change; the moon is simply nearer; moonstone is ordinary ore that happens to glow.
- What is actually true (for the designer, not the player): the land itself is still settling after something larger. The moon’s nearness is real, but the cause is contested even among the Moonwardens. Leave the exact cause in open questions.

## 2. Cosmology & Metaphysics

### The Thinning — Competing Accounts

No single story is accepted. Villagers and Moonwardens keep three main tellings:

1. **The Weather Story (most common):** The sun simply grew thin at the edges over twenty years. Harvests came late, noon shrank to a bright smear, and one winter the noon never whitened. People stopped arguing and changed crops. This is the version taught to children.
2. **The Moonwarden Chart Story:** Old survey-charts show the moon’s path has been creeping closer since Sun Year 40. The Thinning is the land’s answer — valleys sinking, sky pressing down. Moonstone rises because the ground is still adjusting. Wayfinders mark the difference in degrees; scholars argue over the numbers.
3. **The Hearth-Fire Story (old folk only):** Something in the high places went quiet. The sun used to answer the bells; now only the moon answers. The failed manners were the ones that kept shouting at a sky that no longer listened. Keep this as rumor only.

### Nature of the World

- Daylight is grey and short; workable moon-night is the real working day.
- Living nodes (farms, timber, ponds, pastures, mines) rise and fall with the moon’s pull. Overdrawn land goes fallow and pale; rested land glows faintly. Villagers read this the way sailors read weather.
- Ward-light (lamps, watchfires, mooncleric rites) does not harm raiders. It steadies the hands and hearts of defenders. Towers, traps, and walls do the killing.
- No afterlife is claimed. The dead are buried with a lamp that is allowed to burn out. The living keep the lights for the living.

### Open Questions (deliberately unanswered)
- What exactly caused the Thinning?
- Is the moon still approaching, or has it stopped?
- Why does moonstone hold light while ordinary gold does not?
- Do the Hollow Knockers exist, or are they only the sound of the treeline settling?

## 3. Ages of the Long Dusk

Dating: old folk count **Sun Years** before the thinning, and **Dusk Years** after. Year 1 of Dusk is the first winter the noon never whitened. The Manner was founded in Dusk Year 27. Current time is about Dusk Year 48.

### First Age — The Sunlit World (to roughly Sun Year 70)
True noon, tall wheat, soft straight timber. Field terraces and millstones still visible. Hob the ditcher cut the stream that still runs through the bowl valley (Hob’s Cut).

### Second Age — The Thinning (Sun Year 70 to Dusk Year 1)
Sun pale at the edges, then thin all over. Wheat failed. Sella Second-Lantern saved pale barley and moonberry cuttings. Tomm Waterwise taught the valleys to pond fish. Burnt orchards and boarded grey barns remain.

### Third Age — The First Nights (Dusk Year 1 to 27)
Moon swelled near and stayed. Old roads filled with carts going nowhere. Manner after manner raised and lost — too big, too proud, too far from water. Charcoal terraces along the Ember Road. Broken toll-gate at the Timber Line. Failed hearths in the treeline. Pella Bell-founder cast the first ward-bells. Joren Ash’s toll-gang held the western road three winters and starved when the Markets found another way around.

### Fourth Age — Founding of the Manner (Dusk Year 27 to present)
Twenty-one souls followed a Moonwarden survey-chart to Hob’s Cut, raised a hall around Pella’s spare bell, and lit one window. Rule: beds before bodies, walls before pride. The Manner held because it stayed small enough to feed. Maro the mason laid the sill stone of the Manor Hall. The Signal Watchfire has been kept lit since the second raid.

**Lasting consequences still on the map:**
- Field terraces under the east rows.
- Stillwater ponds (Tomm’s line).
- The bent clapper from Pella’s first ringing.
- The tollman’s hat by the hall door.
- Shallow moonstone at Moonwell Hollow.

## 4. Geography & Living Nodes

**Naming pattern:** plain frontier noun + moon/night word. Two words maximum. Concrete and pronounceable.

- **The Manner:** home valley — bowl of fields, stream, treeline. Its old hearth sits inside the 52×44 Outer Frontier, divided into claimable named regions.
- **Stillwater:** pond-chain and stream. Glimmerfish rise to moonlight.
- **The Timber Line:** western treeline where the oldest road enters. New trails now reach the claimed frontier from every side, so raiders probe whichever exposed edge the Manner has pushed into.
- **The Ember Road:** old forge-highway south, now charcoal trail. Expedition country.
- **Moonwell Hollow:** dell where moonstone runs near the surface. Village hungry twice over: bread and ore.
- Far places (named, never shown): Grey Markets, Sunken Steeple, Lantern Towns. Used for notice-board flavor and trader stories only.

Living nodes visibly drain and refill. Overdrawn land goes fallow; rested land glows. This is both visual feedback and lore.

## 5. Factions (Human-Scale Only)

All small. Nobody is evil. Everybody is hungry, tired, or owed.

### The Mannerfolk (us)
Settlers, runaways, second sons, tired soldiers. Twelve frontier professions + the original five. They vote with their feet: surplus food + free beds brings newcomers; hunger or crowding stops them.
- Want: full granary, mended wall, one quiet night in three.
- Hold: the bowl valley, the stream, the Manor bell.
- Soft spot: any failed-manner refugee with tools in hand.

### The Westroad Toll-Gangs
Two crews share the Timber Line by uneasy rota: Kess Longaxe’s polemen and Dren Salt’s carters-turned-tollmen. Mend bridges, charge for passage in moonstone, flour, or labor. When tribute dries, young members freelance as night-probers. They will sell road-news and spare nails at a markup.

### The Ashwood Poachers
Timber crews from two failed clearings. Hearths went cold in Dusk Year 39. Now they cut green timber, lift sheep, pry shallow moonstone. Not hate — arithmetic. Some now tap the Signal Watchfire twice before a raid — a warning of sorts — after the mooncleric ransomed captives with bread.

### The Grey Market Caravans
Traveling traders out of the Lantern Towns. Six wagons, lamp-hung. Never seen on-map. Fair but sharp. Favor the Manner because it pays on time.

### The Lantern Towns
Three walled market towns two days down the road. Lit whole streets. Buy everything, promise nothing. Turned away grandparents in the First Nights; now pay premium for Manner timber.

### The Moonwardens
Scattered wayfinders, scholars, and moonclerics. Keep old survey-charts and bell-codes. Gave the Manner its founding chart and bell-tune. Give quests, never armies. Teach the standing-wall drill; never hold the spear themselves.

**Relationship web (explicit):**
- Toll-gangs tax the Markets.
- Markets pay the Manner.
- Manner feeds poacher captives.
- Poachers warn the watchfire.
- Wardens write it all down and call it peace.
It holds until somebody goes hungry.

## 6. Power System (Small, Costly, Cultural)

- **Ward-light:** lamps, watchfires, mooncleric rites. Steadies defenders. Does not kill.
- **Keeper auras:** a good craftsman posted at a finished workplace sharpens everyone nearby (damage, armor, gather, build, heal, XP, survey). Auras are skill and attention, not spells.
- **Moonstone:** currency, lamp-fuel, and ward-charm. Glows faintly after nightfall. Everyone needs it; nobody has enough. This is why raids happen.
- No fireballs, no resurrection, no free power. Everything costs work, material, or attention.

## 7. Living Characters & Workplace Keepers

Write them as neighbors with jobs, never as classes. Each major workplace has a senior keeper who can speak, remember, and change slightly after major events.

Examples (expand as needed):
- **Mara the mason** — laid half the current walls. Grumbles about green timber. Soft spot for any child who brings her a straight nail.
- **Old Bell the shepherd** — counts by bells, not dogs. Keeps the bent clapper story alive.
- **Second-Lantern Pella** (or her descendant) — seed-keeper line. Still trades in stories of Sella’s box.
- **Maro the mason’s mark** still on the Manor sill.

Every 10th villager arrives with a generated trade-name. Charter rosters list names, not counts.

## 9. Narrative Integration Rules

- Reveal the world through environment, conflicting accounts, daily life, and player actions.
- Quest text: plain speech, one concrete image, one instruction. Two sentences max. Rumor goes in flavor fields.
- Campaign returns must leave fingerprints: resources, unlocks, and small permanent scars or improvements on the home map when possible.
- Notice board is the primary vehicle for ongoing lore, trader prices, and faction soft power.
- Leave negative space. Do not over-define.

## 10. Naming Rules

1. Two words max for places and quests. Frontier noun first, moon-word second.
2. People get trade-names, not titles: *Mara the mason*, *Old Bell*, *Second-Lantern Pella*.
3. Buildings keep the plain data keys. Flavor may add epithets.
4. Never import names from other games, books, or films.
5. Moon-words budget: moon, dusk, lantern, ember, still, long, hollow, signal, ward, pale — rotate, do not stack.

## 11. What the Lore Never Claims

- No return of the sun.
- No gods handing out victories.
- No resurrection (only “dragged home”).
- No real multiplayer, cloud kingdoms, or cross-device realms.
- No dark lords, empires, or world-ending stakes.
- The Grey Markets are fiction for a single-player trading post.
