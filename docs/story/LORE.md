# Midnights Manner — Lore Bible

All original writing for this game. No copied settings, characters, or
place names from other works. If a name sounds familiar, it is coincidence —
rename it.

## The One-Paragraph Setting

The sun went thin a generation ago and never came fully back. In the
**Long Dusk**, the frontier valleys sit under a permanent moonlit night:
crops grow pale and sweet, timber grows hard and blue-veined, and the old
roads belong to whoever holds a lit window. Settlers follow the rumor of
**moonstone** — gold that holds moonlight — and build walled
manners around a single great **Manor Hall**. Our village is one such
manner: one lit window in a dark valley, and keeping it lit is the
whole game.

## Tone

- Quiet frontier hope, not grim horror. Danger is real, but the fantasy is
  *keeping the lights on together*.
- Practical and tactile: bread, timber, nails, watch-rosters, mended walls.
  Magic is rare and small — a blessing on a wall, a charm on a lamp.
  Never a substitute for work.
- Humor is dry and neighborly. Raiders are desperate, not evil incarnate;
  villagers grumble, then show up.
- Nobody gives up. Every defeat ends with salvage and rebuilding, never
  annihilation.

## Core Concepts

- **The Long Dusk:** the moon hangs larger and nearer than it ought to.
  Nights are workable; daylight is grey and thin. Nobody alive
  remembers true noon.
- **Moonstone (gold):** the game resource `gold` in-fiction. that glows faintly after nightfall. Currency, lamp-fuel, and ward-charm in
  one. This is why raiders come: everyone needs it, nobody has enough.
- **The Manor:** the hall building is the village's hearth, granary-vault,
  and bell-tower. If it falls, the village scatters — but crawls back with
  salvaged timber. (Matches the recovery rule: manor falls → raid ends →
  salvage wood.)
- **Living Nodes:** farms, timber stands, ponds, pastures and mines visibly
  drain and refill because the land itself rises and falls with the moon. Overdrawn
  land goes fallow and pale; rested land glows. Villagers read this the way
  sailors read weather.
- **Ward-light:** lamplight, watchfires and the mooncleric's rites. Light
  does not kill raiders — it steadies defenders. Towers, traps and walls do
  the rest.

## Places

Naming pattern: plain frontier noun + moon/night word (e.g. Stillwater
Pond, Moonlit Cottage, Moonberry Grove, Signal Watchfire). Keep names
concrete and pronounceable; two words maximum.

- **The Manner:** our home valley — a bowl of fields, a stream, and a
  treeline. The 20×16 home map.
- **Stillwater:** the pond-chain and stream. Fishers say the fish fell from
  the sky and are still deciding.
- **The Timber Line:** the western treeline where the road enters. Every raid
  comes from the west because that is where the road survives.
- **The Ember Road:** the old forge-highway south, now a charcoal trail.
  Expedition country (mission 04).
- **Moonwell Hollow:** a dell where moonstone runs near the surface.
  The village is hungry twice over: bread for the tables, ore for the
  coffers (mission 05).
- **Far places (named, never shown):** the Grey Markets down the road,
  the Sunken Steeple, the Lantern Towns. Used for notice-board flavor and
  trader origin stories only.

## Factions

All small and human-scale. No empires, no dark lords.

- **The Mannerfolk (us):** settlers, runaways, second sons and tired
  soldiers. Twelve frontier professions plus the original five. They vote
  with their feet: surplus food plus free beds brings newcomers; hunger or
  crowding stops them.
- **The Road Crews (raiders):** not monsters — timber-poachers, toll-gangs,
  and hungry neighbors from failed manners. They probe from the west in
  counted bands, break on walls and towers, and flee when a raid drags too
  long (they lose heart, they do not fight to the last). Defeated village
  troops revive after the raid: the fiction is that our people drag each
  other home.
- **The Grey Markets:** traveling traders. Never seen on-map; they exist as
  trading-post offers and notice-board rumors. Fair but sharp.
- **The Moonwardens (story figures):** a scattered order of wayfinders,
  scholars and moonclerics who keep the old survey-charts and bell-codes.
  They give quests, never armies. The Warden troop is a villager trained in
  their standing-wall drill — not a member of the order.

## Creatures & Oddities

Low-magic, frontier-flavored, all original:

- **Moonberries:** pale fruit that ripens only in moonlight; foragers gather
  them at the Grove.
- **Glimmerfish:** pond fish with star-flecked backs. Fisher's pride.
- **Dusk sheep:** woolly, placid, near-blind in grey daylight; shepherds
  guide them by bell.
- **Moth-deer (rumor):** antlered grazers said to follow the moon's path.
  Mentioned by the wayfinder; never spawned, never required.
- **Hollow knocks (superstition):** raiders chew at walls because, the old
  folk say, something in the treeline knocks back. Nobody has seen it.
  Keep it a rumor — never an enemy type without a design pass.

## Professions (the twelve + five, in-fiction)

Fisher, shepherd, butcher, scholar, wayfinder, mooncleric, smiths, mason,
lumberjack — plus warrior, archer, miner, builder, farmer (and later warden,
ranger, forager). Each belongs to a workplace: the building is their toolshed,
chapel, or pen. Specialists work faster; keepers lend auras because a good
craftsman sharpens everyone around them. Write them as neighbors with jobs,
never as classes with builds.

## Naming Rules

1. Two words max for places and quests. Frontier noun first, moon-word
   second (Timber Line, Moonwell, Ember Road).
2. People get trade-names, not titles: *Mara the mason*, *Old Bell*,
   *Second-Lantern Pella*. No kings, no chosen ones.
3. Buildings keep the plain names in `data/buildings.json` (farm, lumber,
   mine, tower, trap, grove, watchfire). Flavor text may add epithets; data
   keys never change for flavor.
4. Never import names from other games, books, or films. When in doubt,
   pick the plainer word.
5. Moon-words budget: *moon, dusk, lantern, ember, still, long, hollow,
   signal, ward, pale* — rotate, do not stack (no "Moonlit Moon-Moon").

## What the Lore Never Claims

- No return of the sun (that ends the setting).
- No gods handing out victories, no resurrection of the dead — our troops
  *revive* because they were dragged inside, not raised.
- No real multiplayer, no cloud kingdoms, no cross-device realms. The
  Grey Markets are fiction for a single-player trading post.
