# Midnights Manner — Ages of the Long Dusk

All original. A short history the village would actually remember: work, weather, and names on timber.

Dating: old folk count **Sun Years** before the thinning, and **Dusk Years** after. Year 1 of Dusk is the first winter the noon never whitened. The Manner was founded in Dusk Year 27. This telling is set about Dusk Year 48.

## First Age — The Sunlit World (long before, to Sun Year 70 or so)

The valleys were farm country under a true sun. Noon was white and short, bread wheat grew tall, and timber went up soft and straight.

What still shows:

- **Field terraces** under the east rows of the home map — low stone lips in the grass. Farmers still plow around them.
- **The Sunken Steeple** two valleys over — a stone bell-tower sunk to its windows in bog. Traders use it as a milestone: "past the Steeple, turn for the Markets."
- **Millstones** reused as doorsteps all over the Manner. Nobody remembers the mills. Everybody reuses the stone.

Named figure: **Hob the ditcher**, who cut the first drain down the middle of our bowl valley. The stream still follows his line. Old deeds call it Hob's Cut.

## Second Age — The Thinning (Sun Year 70 to Dusk Year 1, about one lifetime)

The sun went pale at the edges, then thin all over. No single day it happened — harvests got late, winters got soft and grey, noon shrank to a bright smear. Folk argued about it for twenty years, then stopped arguing and changed crops.

What still shows:

- **Burnt orchards** on the high slopes — apple and pear that quit bearing. Charcoal crews still dig the stumps for clean fuel.
- **The grey barns** — big sun-crop barns with their wide threshing doors boarded shut. We live in smaller rooms now, nearer the fire.

Named figures: **Sella Second-Lantern**, a seed-keeper who saved pale barley and moonberry cuttings when the wheat failed; and **Tomm Waterwise**, who taught the valleys to pond fish instead of plowing everything.

## Third Age — The First Nights (Dusk Year 1 to Dusk Year 27)

The moon swelled near and stayed. Grey daylight for a few hours, workable moon-night the rest. The old roads filled with carts going nowhere good. Manner after manner was raised and lost — too big, too proud, too far from water.

What still shows:

- **Charcoal terraces** along the Ember Road — flat kiln-pads in rows of ten. That road was a forge-highway once. Now it is a charcoal trail and expedition country.
- **The broken toll-gate** at the Timber Line — two posts and a cut chain where the first toll-gang took coin from refugees. Every raid still comes down that road because it is the only road that survived.
- **Failed hearths** in the treeline — stone squares in the moss, one per clearing. Wayfinders mark them on charts so nobody rebuilds on a dry well.

Named figures: **Pella Bell-founder**, who cast the first ward-bells tuned for night work; and **Joren Ash**, whose toll-gang held the western road for three winters and starved when the Markets found another way around.

## Fourth Age — Founding of the Manner (Dusk Year 27 to now, Year 48)

Twenty-one souls followed a Moonwarden survey-chart to Hob's Cut, raised a hall around Pella's spare bell, and lit one window. The rule was simple: beds before bodies, walls before pride. The Manner held because it stayed small enough to feed.

What still shows — the player's map itself:

- **The Manor Hall** — bell-tower, granary-vault, hearth. Maro the mason laid the sill stone. It still bears his chalk mark.
- **Stillwater ponds** — Tomm's granddaughter dug the first with six neighbors in one night shift.
- **The Signal Watchfire** on the west rise — lit the night the second raid broke, and kept lit since.
- **Moonwell Hollow** — found when a shepherd's bell-rod rang against shallow moonstone. The village has been hungry twice over ever since: bread for the tables, ore for the coffers.

The Manner's lesson, chalked inside the hall door: *Keep one light. Mend the rest.*
