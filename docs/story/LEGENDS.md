# Midnights Manner — Legends of the Manner

Told at tables, pinned to the notice board, read while the map loads. One paragraph each. All villagers swear at least one is true.

## 1. Hob's Cut

They say Hob the ditcher dug our stream with a spade, a lamp, and a grudge against standing water, and when the surveyors told him the line was crooked he said the water didn't mind, and neither did he. The stream still runs his line. So do the arguments.

## 2. The Night the Bell Learned

The first winter, wolves — no, raiders, no, wind — something worried the sheep, and young Pella hung her father's cracked field-bell in the hall frame and rang it till the clapper bent. Every soul in the bowl answered with lamp and pitchfork and found nothing but sheep, sheep, and one bewildered shepherd. Pella wept. Old Bell keeps the bent clapper on the market stall and charges children a button to touch it for luck.

## 3. Sella's Seeds

When the wheat failed, Sella Second-Lantern carried her seed box through three dead manners, trading bread for handfuls of whatever still grew, and on the Grey Market road a tollman demanded the box itself. She opened it, showed him dust and shriveled skins, and said eat, then, if you're hungrier than spring. He let her pass, and took off his hat besides. Half our barley is descended from that box.

## 4. The Tollman's Hat

Joren Ash held the western road three winters and taxed every cart, every mule, every widow with a bundle, until the Lantern Towns found another way around and his own men ate his horses. The last toll taken on the Timber Line was his own hat, thrown down in the mud when he walked into our hall and asked for work. Maro the mason gave him a hod and a bed. The hat hangs by the door. Nobody touches it. Everybody checks that it's still there.

## 5. The Shepherd's Find

A shepherd girl — name changes with the telling — dropped her bell-rod down Moonwell Hollow chasing a stray, heard it ring wrong on stone, and dug with her hands till moonlight came up out of the dirt like water. She ran home shouting my rod's gone gold, and the village has been hungry twice over ever since: bread for the tables, ore for the coffers. The rod hangs in the Manor. The stray came home on its own.
