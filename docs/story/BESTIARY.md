# Midnights Manner — Bestiary of the Moonlit Wilds

How to read the wilds: look for the sign before the thing. Villagers learn tracks, sounds, and water first, teeth never.

## Glimmerfish (Stillwater ponds)

Star-flecked backs, fat as a hand. Rise to moonlight to feed.

- **Habit:** school slow circles under the pond mirror; scatter at boot-shake on the bank.
- **Sign one is near:** the water winks — small cold flashes just under the surface. Fishers say the pond is thinking.
- **Use:** fisher's pride, feast fish. Harmless. If the pond goes dark for nights running, the node is overdrawn — rest it.

## Dusk Sheep (pastures)

Woolly, placid, near-blind in grey daylight. Led by bell, not dog.

- **Habit:** graze facing the moon, drift downhill as the night wears on.
- **Sign one is near:** bell-clacks in threes, and wool tufts on every fence nail.
- **Use:** mutton, wool, patience lessons for shepherds. Harmless. Count bells at dusk; a missing bell means a gap in the fence, not a monster.

## Moon-Moths (groves and lamp rims)

Pale wings wide as a palm, dusted like frost. Follow moonberry bloom.

- **Habit:** gather thick where moonberries ripen; thin the hour before a raid the way birds hush — not magic, just shy of pole-oil and cut pine.
- **Sign one is near:** wing-dust on lamp glass, and a soft papery hush over the grove.
- **Use:** none but beauty and warning. Foragers read a heavy moth-night as a good picking night. Harmless.

## Lantern Crows (Manor roof, watchfire)

Black birds with one white tail feather, clever as tax collectors.

- **Habit:** steal lamp wicks, nails, fish heads; mob owls at grey dawn.
- **Sign one is near:** a laughed *kak-kak* from the roof ridge, and missing wicks in the morning.
- **Use:** pest and postman both — the wayfinder ties thread-markers to their legs to find lost sheep. Harmless, except to wicks.

## Bark Owls (Timber Line)

Small grey owls that sound like knuckles on wood.

- **Habit:** hunt mice along the treeline; go silent when men move with poles and oil.
- **Sign one is near:** steady knock-knock-knock from three trees at a time. When all three stop at once, something bigger is walking the road.
- **Use:** the watch's truest alarm. Old Bell says: owls first, bell second. Harmless.

## Moth-Deer (rumor, high clearings)

Antlered grazers said to follow the moon's path across the high meadows. Mentioned by the wayfinder; never penned, never hunted in-story.

- **Habit:** graze in single file, heads all one way.
- **Sign one is near:** dew flattened in a long curve, and moon-moths rising where nothing seems to have passed.
- **Use:** story-meat. Seeing one is good luck for a year, which the butcher says is easy to promise since nobody sees one twice. Harmless.

## Bristle Boars (embers and mast)

Grizzled rooters in the ash woods south, fat on fallen mast.

- **Habit:** turn soil and charcoal together; sows bluff-charge, then think better of it.
- **Sign one is near:** fresh plow-lines in the leaf mold, and a smell like hot iron and mushrooms.
- **Use:** real meat behind real risk — the butcher's prize and the mason's complaint, since they undermine kiln-pads. Shy of ward-light. A nuisance, not a raid.

## Hollow Knockers (superstition — never an enemy)

Something in the treeline knocks back when raiders chew at walls. Nobody has seen it. Keep it that way.

- **Habit:** unknown. That is the whole entry, and the village likes it so.
- **Sign one is near:** two knocks answered by two knocks, wood on wood, from deeper in than the raiders stand.
- **Use:** grandmothers use it to hurry children home. Watchmen use it to stay awake. Never spawned, never required, never explained without a full design pass.
